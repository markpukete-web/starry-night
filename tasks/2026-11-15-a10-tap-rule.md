## The rule

A press is a tap when all of these hold:

- It started a gesture: a finger, a pen, or a mouse's button 0, from the primary pointer (`startsGesture`).
- No sample of any move went more than `tapPx` (8 CSS px) from the press. Every coalesced sample counts (`moveSamples`,
  `strayed`).
- No second pointer went down while it was held.
- Its release is within `tapPx` of the press and within `tapMs` (300 ms) of it, both timed by the events' `timeStamp`.

Anything else is a drag. `classifyGesture` in `touchSurge.ts` takes a `Press`, whose `dragged` field holds the latch.
The field is required, so a caller cannot leave it out. The browser checks pin that each hook sets it.

The lifecycle, the same in the three hooks:

- A primary press while one is held means the old release was lost. The new press replaces it. In the sky's hook the
  old press's land hold is released first (`releaseLand`). In the street the old gesture is cleared before the target
  test, so a press on the dock leaves the grass no stale gesture.
- A second finger marks the held press a drag. The street clears the gesture instead, as before.
- A `pointercancel` drops the press, as before. The portal still drops it on any pointer's cancel. That can only lose
  a tap, so it stays.

No threshold changed. The Lead's calls: build all three surfaces, keep the stale-press replacement in all three, add no
`visibilitychange` listener to the portal (the replacement closes that gap).

name, among them the wander's own `m-f2` and `m-f2-folded`, now on the shared rule. On the first full run, 7d threw

mutant 10 again with that fix: 7d fails on its own assert.
