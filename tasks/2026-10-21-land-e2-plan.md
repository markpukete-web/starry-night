## What the painting's land is (the 1:1 crops)

- **The fields** under and around the village (v 0.78–1.0): dark blue-green and navy ground in long, mostly flat
  strokes, 15–30 px wide at 3840, 100–400 px long; a band of dark teal foliage with pale curling strokes
  behind the houses; the houses' walls stand on it.
- **The hills** (v 0.60–0.78): blue-violet rolling masses in diagonal strokes leaning 40–60° from vertical, the
  big hill at the right, the foot dissolving into the fields.
- **The olive trees**: rounded masses of curling teal and blue strokes, each a cluster of arcs 150–250 px wide
  (0.04–0.06 of the width), in two rows along the right hill's foot (u 0.74–1.0, v 0.70–0.82) and singly among
  the houses (behind the church, left of the striped roof); dark green-blue with pale blue highlights.
- **The bushes** at the cypress's foot (u 0.02–0.2, v 0.86–1.0): yellow-green curls outlined in blue, 100–200 px
  blobs, on the ground in front of the trunk.

## Measured, 2026-10-21

**The mapping cannot come from the camera's rays.** For a grid of island-top points, the home camera's ray into
the sky's frame (`homeInverseQuaternion`, `uvFromSkyDirection`) lands on u 0.39–0.72, v 0.635–0.742: the island's
whole top covers the painting's hills band only, and the painting's village (u 0.56, v 0.87) and fields (v 0.97)
project onto the island's root (y −0.35, −0.77) or miss it; the bushes at the cypress's foot (u 0.1, v 0.95) miss.
So the island's top takes the land band by a plan-view map instead, the way the village's houses are already
placed: the church stands at world x 0.08 under the painting's u 0.56 and the rows span 4.4 world units per unit
of u (`villageGeometry.ts`); the apron in front of the village's back row (z 1.35 → 0.25) carries the fields
(v 1.0 → 0.78), the humps behind (z 0.25 → −1.35) carry the hills (v 0.78 → 0.60). By that map the orchard (u 0.87,
v 0.75) lands at x 1.44 on the right hump's foot, the bushes (u 0.1, v 0.95) at x −1.94, z 1.1 in front of the
cypress's base, the F2 rows where they stand.

**The land band's field at a third of the scan** (1280 × 426, σ 2, low-pass 2, the cypress column excluded
above v 0.95): mean anisotropy 0.258. Anisotropy-weighted share by angle from vertical: the hills 40–60° 40%,
60–90° 37%, under 30° 13% (the diagonal hatching); the fields 80–90° 20%, 40–60° 22%, spread (flat strokes and
the foliage's curls). The fallback for texels with no orientation must be horizontal for the land (the
cypress's is vertical).

**Colour classes in the band** (the same rules the derive uses, E2.1): dark ground, teal curls, blue hill, and
yellow-green bushes each a measurable share; the painting's foliage masses separate from the fields by
luminance (the teal curls' 35–110 against the ground's under 45) and the bushes by hue.

## Design

### E2.1 — the derivation (`scripts/derive-land.ts`, `scripts/lib/cypress-field.ts`)

From `reference/derived/cypress-work.png` (the 3840 scan's decode, E1's; `derive-land` runs the same `sips` step
if it is missing) take the band v ∈ [0.58, 1.0] (rows 1763–3040) at a third of the resolution: 1280 × 426,
box-filtered. `cypress-field.ts`'s `blendToVertical` takes the fallback as a parameter:

```ts
/** The double-angle of the fallback orientation: vertical (the cypress) or horizontal (the land). */
export const VERTICAL: readonly [number, number] = [-1, 0]
export const HORIZONTAL: readonly [number, number] = [1, 0]

export function blendToVertical(cos2, sin2, anisotropy, fallback: readonly [number, number] = VERTICAL)
```

(`signedUpDirection` resolves `[1, 0]` to `dx 1, dy 0`, a stroke along the ground; `[-1, 0]` to `dy −1`.)

The field: `orientationField(luminance, bandMask, 2)` (σ 2 at a third is σ 6 at the scan), `lowPassOrientation`
σ 2, `blendToVertical(…, HORIZONTAL)`. `bandMask` is 1 everywhere except the cypress column above v 0.95
(u < 0.26), where the island's top never maps and the tree's strokes would leak into the ground.

The masses: every pixel of the band classed by colour, the two foliage classes closed by 1 px and labelled:

```ts
export type LandClass = 'teal' | 'yellowGreen' | 'ground' | 'hill' | 'other'
/** The band's paint by its colour: the teal curls are the trees and hedges, the yellow-green curls the bushes. */
export function landClass(r: number, g: number, b: number): LandClass {
  const l = 0.299 * r + 0.587 * g + 0.114 * b
  if (g > r + 25 && g > b + 10 && l > 70) return 'yellowGreen'
  if (g >= r && b >= r && l >= 35 && l < 110 && Math.abs(g - b) < 40) return 'teal'
  if (l < 45) return 'ground'
  if (b > g + 10 && b > r + 20) return 'hill'
  return 'other'
}
```

A component of `teal` or `yellowGreen` pixels with at least 400 px at 1280 (36 px wide) is a mass. The
derive writes `public/reference/land-masses.json`: per mass `{ id, class, u0, v0, u1, v1, pixels, runs }` with
`runs` its row runs in band-local pixels (for the mass's own mask at runtime), sorted by pixels. The guard: the
F2 rows' stations must each fall inside a teal mass (front u 0.46–0.52, back 0.63–0.76, right 0.74–0.80, left
0.43–0.47; v from the stations' crests), at least two teal masses at u > 0.74 and v < 0.82 (the orchard), and at
least one yellow-green mass at u < 0.2 (the bushes); otherwise the derive throws with the list it found.

Outputs: `public/reference/land-flow.png` (1280 × 426: R, G the signed direction; B `max(1, anisotropy × 255)`
inside the band mask, 0 in the cypress column; A 255), `public/reference/land-masses.json`,
`reference/derived/land-field-overlay.png` (the ticks over the band, green by anisotropy) and
`land-masses-overlay.png` (the masses outlined by class). Sizes measured in the gate (a bar of 500 KB for the
PNG).

Tests (`scripts/land-field.test.ts`): `landClass` on the five classes' exemplars from the crops; `blendToVertical`
with `HORIZONTAL` keeps a 30° texel at anisotropy 1 and resolves an anisotropy-0 texel to `dx 1`; the shipped
`land-masses.json` holds the guard's masses; the shipped `land-flow.png` has B 0 in the cypress column and B ≥ 1
elsewhere, and its angle histogram in the hills' rows peaks between 40° and 60°.

### E2.2 — the plan-view map (`src/scene/landMapping.ts`, new, pure)

```ts
import { RX, RZ } from './islandShape.ts'

/**
 * The painting's land band on the island's top (E2): the apron in front of the village's back row carries the
 * fields, the humps behind carry the hills, x across the island is u at the village's own scale (the church at
 * world x 0.08 under u 0.56; the houses span 4.4 world units per unit of u, villageGeometry's rows). The home
 * camera's rays cannot give this map: through the dome the island's top lands on the hills only (the plan's
 * "Measured").
 */
export const LAND_MAP = {
  u0: 0.56,
  x0: 0.08,
  worldPerU: 4.4,
  /** z from the front rim to the village's back row ↔ v from the painting's bottom to the fields' top */
  apron: { zFront: RZ, zBack: 0.25, vFront: 1.0, vBack: 0.78 },
  /** the humps ↔ the hills */
  hills: { zBack: -RZ, vBack: 0.6 },
  /** the band the flow asset covers */
  bandV0: 0.58,
} as const

const lerp = (a: number, b: number, t: number) => a + (b - a) * t

/** Painting uv under an island-top point. */
export function paintingUvAt(x: number, z: number): [number, number] {
  const { u0, x0, worldPerU, apron, hills } = LAND_MAP
  const u = u0 + (x - x0) / worldPerU
  const v =
    z >= apron.zBack
      ? lerp(apron.vBack, apron.vFront, (z - apron.zBack) / (apron.zFront - apron.zBack))
      : lerp(hills.vBack, apron.vBack, (z - hills.zBack) / (apron.zBack - hills.zBack))
  return [u, v]
}

/** The island-top point under a painting uv (the inverse). */
export function worldAt(u: number, v: number): [number, number] {
  const { u0, x0, worldPerU, apron, hills } = LAND_MAP
  const x = x0 + (u - u0) * worldPerU
  const z =
    v >= apron.vBack
      ? lerp(apron.zBack, apron.zFront, (v - apron.vBack) / (apron.vFront - apron.vBack))
      : lerp(hills.zBack, apron.zBack, (v - hills.vBack) / (apron.vBack - hills.vBack))
  return [x, z]
}

/** A land-flow sample (crop-normalised px, py) as painting uv. */
export function flowToPainting(px: number, py: number): [number, number] {
  return [px, LAND_MAP.bandV0 + py * (1 - LAND_MAP.bandV0)]
}

/** World units per painting unit at a point: the local scale of the map, for widths. */
export function landScaleAt(z: number): { perU: number; perV: number } {
  const { worldPerU, apron, hills } = LAND_MAP
  const perV = z >= apron.zBack ? (apron.zFront - apron.zBack) / (apron.vFront - apron.vBack) : (apron.zBack - hills.zBack) / (apron.vBack - hills.vBack)
  return { perU: worldPerU, perV }
}
```

Tests (`scripts/land-mapping.test.ts`): `worldAt(paintingUvAt(x, z))` round-trips over the island; the church's
(0.08, 1.0) maps near u 0.56; the F2 rows' world x0 map inside their stations' u spans; v is monotone in z and
continuous at `apron.zBack`; the bushes' uv lands left of the cypress's base and in front of it.

Each ribbon's samples (land-flow px, py) go through `flowToPainting` then `worldAt` to the island's top; the
strip's columns (`ribbonColumns`, `STRIP_COLUMNS`) the same, each on its own, lifted along the top's normal:

```ts
/** The island's top at a plan-view point with its normal from finite differences; null beyond the coast. */
export function terrainAt(x: number, z: number): { position: Vector3; normal: Vector3 } | null {
  const ang = Math.atan2(z, x)
  if (Math.hypot(x, z) > coastR(ang) * 0.995) return null
  const y = islandHeightAt(x, z)
  const eps = 0.02
  const normal = new Vector3(-(islandHeightAt(x + eps, z) - y) / eps, 1, -(islandHeightAt(x, z + eps) - y) / eps).normalize()
  return { position: new Vector3(x, y, z), normal }
}

/**
 * A ribbon's strip on the island's top: every column point mapped and lifted on its own (E1's drape with the
 * terrain in place of the lobes' union). The seed anchors the kept run; a column beyond the coast ends it above
 * the seed, restarts it below, and a seed beyond the coast means no run.
 */
export function drapeOnTerrain(ribbon: CypressRibbon, columns: [number, number][][], lift: number): { steps: Vector3[][]; kept: number } | null {
  const seedIndex = ribbon.samples.indexOf(ribbon.seedSample)
  const steps: Vector3[][] = []
  for (let k = 0; k < ribbon.samples.length; k++) {
    const points: Vector3[] = []
    for (const [px, py] of columns[k]) {
      const [x, z] = worldAt(...flowToPainting(px, py))
      const hit = terrainAt(x, z)
      if (!hit) break
      points.push(hit.position.addScaledVector(hit.normal, lift))
    }
    if (points.length !== columns[k].length) {
      if (k === seedIndex) return null
      if (k < seedIndex) { steps.length = 0; continue }
      break
    }
    steps.push(points)
  }
  return steps.length >= 3 ? { steps, kept: steps.length } : null
}
```

`LAND_LIFT` 0.02 (the old dabs sat at 0.025). The widths come out of the map: a half-width of 0.006 band widths
is 0.006 × 4.4 = 0.026 world units across, the painting's stroke at the village's scale; along z the apron's
scale is 5.0 world units per unit of v, so a stroke's foreshortening is the island's.

`BrushIsland` keeps its solid and its root dabs. The top's 7,200 contour dabs go; the solid's `topBase` darkens
to the fields' underpaint (`PALETTE.hills` × 0.55: the gaps read as shadowed ground). The ground ribbons are
their own mesh at `renderOrder` 1 with the cypress's stroke material: `MeshBasicMaterial({ vertexColors, toneMapped: false, side: DoubleSide, alphaTest: 0.5 })`
and `injectAlive(material, concatInjections('land-strokes', { uniforms: {}, ...strokeGlsl }))`, the attributes
`aStroke`, `aSeed` as the cypress packs them. The pigment is the plain band mean (`accept` everything): the hills'
blue is paint too.

Tests (`scripts/land-strokes.test.ts`): `terrainAt` null beyond the coast, the normal unit and up-facing;
`drapeOnTerrain` on a fixture ribbon across the coast (the run ends/restarts/returns null by the seed's side);
on the real `land-flow.png` 1,200 ribbons return at least 1,000 draped strips with every step inside the coast;
the strips' lift: every point at least 0.5 × `LAND_LIFT` above `islandHeightAt` at its own (x, z) and no more than
2 × above.

### E2.4 — the trees and bushes (`src/scene/landMasses.ts`, `LandTrees.tsx`, new)

Each mass in `land-masses.json` becomes a stroked volume where the map puts it:

```ts
export type LandMass = { id: string; class: 'teal' | 'yellowGreen'; u0: number; v0: number; u1: number; v1: number; pixels: number; runs: { y: number; x0: number; x1: number }[] }

/** world units per painting pixel for a form's height: the houses' 0.0038 (villageGeometry) */
export const HEIGHT_PER_PX = 0.0038

export type Dome = { centre: Vector3; rx: number; ry: number; rz: number; mass: LandMass; s0: number; s1: number }

/**
 * A mass as a row of domes along its base: one dome per `ry × 1.4` of width, overlapping by a third, each as
 * tall as the mass (its v extent in painting pixels) and as deep as half its width. The painting's rows of
 * curls read as bumps, and a single olive as one dome.
 */
export function domesOf(mass: LandMass): Dome[] {
  const [x0, zBase] = worldAt(mass.u0, mass.v1)
  const [x1] = worldAt(mass.u1, mass.v1)
  const height = (mass.v1 - mass.v0) * 1267 * HEIGHT_PER_PX
  const width = x1 - x0
  const ry = height / 2
  const count = Math.max(1, Math.round(width / (ry * 1.4)))
  const domes: Dome[] = []
  for (let i = 0; i < count; i++) {
    const s0 = i / count
    const s1 = (i + 1) / count
    const cx = x0 + width * (s0 + s1) / 2
    const rx = (width / count) * 0.75
    const ground = islandHeightAt(cx, zBase)
    domes.push({ centre: new Vector3(cx, ground + ry * 0.9, zBase), rx, ry, rz: Math.min(rx, ry) * 0.5, mass, s0, s1 })
  }
  return domes
}

/**
 * The mass's picture (s across its box 0..1, t down 0..1) lifted onto a dome's front or back: the ellipsoid's
 * surface at that across and height, the rim plane beyond its silhouette (E1's tongues), null past 0.6 of a
 * radius beyond. The map is a parallel projection of the painting's patch onto the dome, so the painting's
 * curls land where they are painted.
 */
export function liftToDome(dome: Dome, s: number, t: number, front: boolean): { position: Vector3; lateral: number } | null {
  const across = ((s - dome.s0) / (dome.s1 - dome.s0)) * 2 - 1
  const up = (1 - t) * 2 - 1
  const r2 = across * across + up * up
  const over = Math.sqrt(r2) - 1
  if (over > 0.6) return null
  const depth = r2 < 1 ? Math.sqrt(1 - r2) : 0
  const facing = front ? 1 : -1
  return { position: new Vector3(dome.centre.x + across * dome.rx, dome.centre.y + up * dome.ry, dome.centre.z + facing * depth * dome.rz), lateral: Math.sqrt(r2) }
}
```

The cladding per mass: a flow copy the size of the mass's box with B kept on its own `runs` and zeroed elsewhere,
so `traceCypressRibbons` seeds inside the mass and overshoots its edge by `overshootSteps` 3 (the curls break
the silhouette); the config `MASS_RIBBON_CONFIG`: count by area (`pixels / 90`, at least 24, at most 220),
lengths 0.15–0.45 of the box's height, step 2 px, half-widths 0.03–0.06 of the box's width (the arcs are 15–30
px wide at 3840 on a 150–250 px mass), taper 0.5. Each sample `(px, py)` of the box → `(s, t)` → the dome whose
`[s0, s1]` holds s → `liftToDome` front; the strip's columns likewise (E1's drape pattern, with the dome in place
of the lobes). The back takes a second trace at 0.4 of the count on the back face. The lift 0.012 along the
dome's outward normal at the point (`(x − cx)/rx², …` normalised). The pigment: the band mean with the teal or
yellow-green class as the filter (the mass's own paint, not the house wall beside it).

`LandTrees` builds one geometry for every mass's strips, the cypress's stroke material (`land-trees` key), no
alive attribute. The domes' solids: one `SphereGeometry`-free underpaint per dome (a 10 × 8 ellipsoid from
`BufferGeometry`, the class's darkest texel colour) so the gaps between strokes read as shadow and the volume
survives the orbit, drawn at `renderOrder` 2 before the strips at 3.

The village's four foliage rows go: `ROWS`, `foliageRow` and the `foliage` mesh leave `villageGeometry.ts` and
`Village.tsx`; `scripts/export-village.mjs` asserts the three remaining meshes. Their places are the teal
masses the derive finds (the guard in E2.1 requires each row's stations inside one).

Tests (`scripts/land-masses.test.ts`): `domesOf` gives one dome for a mass as wide as tall and a row for a wide
one, every dome's base on the terrain; `liftToDome` on the surface inside, the rim plane beyond, null past 0.6;
the front and back mirror in z; on the shipped masses every mass yields at least 24 draped front strips and the
orchard's two at least 60 each; no strip point lower than its dome's base.
